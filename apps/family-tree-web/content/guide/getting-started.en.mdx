## 1. Start with what you know

Add yourself, your parents, siblings and grandparents. If you don't know a date, enter **just the year**, or mark it **"about"** or **"before"**. An honest approximate date is better than a wrong exact one.

## 2. Interview your elders

A phone recording is the easiest start — use the app's **audio recorder** and attach it to the right person. Questions to ask:

- Who were your parents and grandparents? Where were they born and raised?
- What is our **clan name** and **ethnic group**? Where is our homeland?
- Tell me about your childhood, moves, and work.
- Does the family keep **photos, letters, medals**?
- What **message** would you like to leave for the future generations?

## 3. Collect documents

- Birth and marriage certificates, copies of ID cards
- **Work record books**, military service books, certificates for medals
- Old photos and letters — **scan them** in high quality and keep the originals somewhere dry and dark.

## 4. Search the archives

The National Archives of Mongolia, aimag archives and the civil registration authority may hold old registers, census records and personnel files. Check their access rules before visiting.

## 5. Invite your relatives

A family tree is **teamwork**. Invite relatives by link or **QR code** and give them editor rights — each of them can complete their own branch.

## 6. Think about privacy

You can keep living people's phone numbers, addresses and exact birthdays visible to the family only. If you make a tree public, **sensitive details of living people are hidden automatically**.
