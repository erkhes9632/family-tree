Mongolian families traditionally do not cut a child's **birth hair** until a special ceremony a few years after birth. The first haircut marks a step into childhood and brings the extended family together to bless the child.

## At what age?

Commonly **boys at an odd age** (3 or 5) and **girls at an even age** (2 or 4). Many families choose an auspicious day from the lunar calendar, sometimes with advice from a lama or astrologer.

## How it is done

1. The parents tie a **khadag** to a pair of scissors and place them on a plate.
2. An **elder whose zodiac year suits the child** cuts the first lock and speaks a blessing (*yerööl*).
3. Guests, from the most senior down, each cut a small lock, **say a blessing and give a gift** — livestock, money, books.
4. The hair is wrapped in a **khadag or clean cloth** and kept by the family.
5. The feast includes ul boov, white foods and other festive dishes.

## Record it in your family tree

Add the ceremony as an **Event** on the child's profile — the date, who made the first cut, the blessings that were said — and attach the photos and video. Years later it becomes one of the most treasured memories.
