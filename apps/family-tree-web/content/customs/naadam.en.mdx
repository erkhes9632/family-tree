Naadam is Mongolia's great national festival of the **"three games of men"**: wrestling, horse racing and archery. The national Naadam is held every year on **11–13 July**, and every aimag and sum holds its own. Naadam was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010.

## Wrestling (bökh)

- Wrestlers wear the **zodog** jacket and **shuudag** briefs and perform the **eagle dance** before and after a bout.
- Bouts are knockout rounds; a wrestler loses when his **back, elbow or knee** touches the ground.
- Titles include **nachin** (falcon), **zaan** (elephant), **arslan** (lion) and **avarga** (titan); the title system has changed over time.
- Each wrestler's herald (*zasuul*) sings his titles and praises.

## Horse racing

Horses race by age class — **daaga** (2-year-olds), **shüdlen**, **khyazaalan**, **soyolon**, **ikh nas** (adults) and **azarga** (stallions). Trainers prepare horses for weeks, and the riders are traditionally children. Winning horses are honoured with poems and sprinkled with airag.

## Archery

Men and women compete. Judges sing out **"uukhai"** when an arrow hits. Since 1998 **knucklebone shooting** (shagai kharvaa) has been part of the national Naadam; it was inscribed by UNESCO in 2014.

## Naadam and family history

Many families remember that "grandfather was an aimag zaan" or "great-grandfather's horse won". Record such titles and medals under **Awards & achievements** on the person's profile, and keep certificates and photos in the family archive.
