In Mongolian tradition a marriage joins not only two people but **two families and two lineages** (*urag barildakh*). It unfolds in several steps.

## 1. The request

Senior relatives of the groom — often his father, uncle or a respected elder — visit the bride's family carrying a **khadag, a snuff bottle and gifts**. They state their purpose **indirectly, through allegory**. If the bride's parents agree, they accept the khadag; if not, they return it politely.

## 2. The engagement

The families meet to agree the wedding day and gifts. From now on the two sets of parents are **khud** (co-fathers-in-law) and **khudgui** (co-mothers-in-law). Traditionally the groom's side provides a ger and livestock while the bride's family prepares her **inj** (dowry).

## 3. The wedding

The bride arrives at her new home, **bows to the hearth**, greets the elders and is introduced to her husband's relatives. A blessing-singer (*yerööch*) recites wishes, with songs and music.
